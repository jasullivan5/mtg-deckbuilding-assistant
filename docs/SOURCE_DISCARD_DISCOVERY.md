# Discovery of costs that discard the source card

The application could analyze an activation cost that asks you to choose a card to discard. It left the SDK cost for discarding the exact card with the ability unsupported. This hid creature-card arrivals and obscured the difference between discarding a creature and discarding a land that later creates creature tokens.

## Expectations from the rules and card sources

In the Comprehensive Rules effective November 6, 2026, 201.5 covers self-reference, 602.2 and 601.2c/i put legal target selection before payment of the activation cost, and 701.9a moves a discarded card from its owner's hand to that player's graveyard. Rule 700.4 requires a battlefield departure for a death. Replacement effects can change the destination under 614.6. The Kamigawa Channel rulings confirm that discard pays for activation and that required targets must be legal. The relevant comparison is the original card in the owner's graveyard at the checkpoint. A failed activation or a replacement can leave that checkpoint unsatisfied. ## What changed by layer

- **Source and identity.** The reviewed native cards match the physical Oracle records above by name, type, relevant ability and set/collector number. Discovery still filters by effective Commander metadata. An association to a printing establishes source identity for this comparison.
- **Native representation and authored translation.** The selected actions use `AbilityCost.DiscardSelf` inside the activation cost, `activateFromZone = HAND`, and separate mana and effect nodes. Target requirements live on the action. `CostHandler` checks the source in hand and passes that specific entity to `ZoneTransitionService.discardCard`. For the cases reviewed, this represents the discard instruction. Other portions of the authored abilities and the wider catalog retain their separate evidence limits. [SDK and engine at the resolved revision](https://github.com/jasullivan5/argentum-engine/tree/03869c08665bf476a8b40dacbfbcd501c54a33bd)
- **Extraction and objective.** The recurring defect was the absent source-bound movement fact. The existing walker now emits one `MOVE_OBJECT` fact with a self binding, an exact count of one, the owner's hand and nominal graveyard, and the original activation action. The existing creature-card and zone compatibility query evaluates that fact. Unsupported face characteristics stay `UNKNOWN`. Inconsistent activation zones retain an unsupported record. Other cost mechanisms, such as keyword cycling, have their own native representations and remain outside this correction.
- **Presentation.** The existing inspector explains that the source creature card pays for the activation, preserves timing and target requirements, and distinguishes the discard from a death. A source discard of a noncreature identifies the object mismatch. Engine observations below are bounded tests. They leave the independently reviewed translation ledger and historical failures intact.

## Candidate selection and follow-up

Source-bound discard had a recurring, exact native representation and a direct route to a rules-derived result with few moving parts. Source-bound sacrifice also recurs, but resolving it across triggered effects and activations requires tracking the same object, its battlefield controller, its owner and its creature status after the move. Variable mill quantities, for example on Dread Summons, also recur, but broadening the positive-count rule risks joining an available quantity to the wrong player or branch. The existing evidence makes source-bound self-sacrifice the next bounded candidate. Start with Mogg Fanatic and a noncreature or animated-source control using the existing native arrival evaluator.

The historical Tatterkite collection-counter failure remains a separate execution and assurance case. The archived observation described a native prohibition that the then-pinned engine violated on collection counter placement. This increment makes no assurance transition for that case.
